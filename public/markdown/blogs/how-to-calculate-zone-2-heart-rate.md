## **What Heart Rate Is Zone 2?**

Zone 2 sits at roughly **60% to 70% of your maximum heart rate**. That percentage range is the constant. Everything below is about how you calculate the maximum heart rate that percentage gets applied to.

## **Step 1: Calculate Your Maximum Heart Rate**

You cannot calculate Zone 2 heart rate without this number first. Three formulas dominate:

**Fox Formula (1971):** Max HR \= 220 − Age. The oldest and most recognizable method, but it was never based on a formal research study. It tends to overestimate max heart rate in older adults and underestimate it in younger ones.

**Tanaka Formula (2001):** Max HR \= 208 − (0.7 × Age). Built from a meta-analysis of over 18,000 people and published in the ***Journal of the American College of Cardiology***. This is the formula most modern calculators, including ours, use by default.

**Maffetone "180 Formula":** Max Aerobic HR \= 180 − Age, then adjusted up or down based on training history and health status. Popularized by coach Phil Maffetone, this method skips maximum heart rate entirely and estimates your aerobic ceiling directly. It runs more conservatively than the percentage-based methods for most people.

For a 40-year-old, the difference matters: Fox gives 180 bpm max, Tanaka gives 180 bpm max too at this particular age (a coincidence in the math), but the gap widens noticeably at both younger and older ages.

## **Step 2: Apply the Zone 2 Percentage**

Once you have a maximum heart rate, calculating Zone 2 is simple multiplication.

Zone 2 Lower Bound \= Max HR × 0.60

Zone 2 Upper Bound \= Max HR × 0.70

**Worked example, age 40, Tanaka formula:** Max HR \= 208 − (0.7 × 40\) \= 180 Lower bound \= 180 × 0.60 \= 108 Upper bound \= 180 × 0.70 \= 126 Zone 2 range \= **108–126 bpm**

## **Step 3 (More Precise): The Karvonen Method**

If you know your resting heart rate, the Karvonen formula personalizes the result instead of relying on age alone.

Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) \= Max HR − Resting HR

Zone 2 Target \= (HRR × 0.50 to 0.60) \+ Resting HR

**Worked example, age 40, resting HR of 55:** HRR \= 180 − 55 \= 125\. Lower bound \= (125 × 0.50) \+ 55 \= 118\. Upper bound \= (125 × 0.60) \+ 55 \= 130\. Zone 2 range \= **118–130 bpm**

Notice this range runs higher than the simple percentage method. A lower resting heart rate usually signals better cardiovascular fitness, and the Karvonen formula rewards that with a higher sustainable ceiling.

## **Mistakes That Throw Off the Calculation**

**Using 220 − age and treating it as exact:** It is a rough starting point, not a lab-verified number. Real max heart rate can vary by 10–15 bpm from the formula's estimate in either direction.

**Ignoring resting heart rate when you have it:** If you own a fitness tracker, you already have this number. Skipping the Karvonen step means leaving accuracy on the table for free.

**Forgetting that fitness level shifts the real-world number:** Two people with identical formula results can have different true aerobic thresholds based on training history, which is why the talk test remains a useful gut check against any formula.

**Applying one static number forever:** Maximum heart rate drifts slightly with age, so it is worth recalculating every year or two, especially after a significant change in fitness or health status.

## **How Do I Know If I'm in Zone 2?**

Beyond the number on your wrist, two checks confirm you are there: you can hold a full conversation without gasping, and you could sustain the effort for 45+ minutes without your form or energy collapsing. If either check fails, the number on the screen does not matter yet.
